# MF 6.1

## Scope and provenance

The MF 6.1 model (`FITTYP = 61`) ports the MF612 formulation supplied by Pedro Stella from `movedot/movedot-tire-solver`, revision `f9bd2e8`. Its source is `src/ModelImplementations/MF612.cpp`; its accompanying ISO tire fixture and six value cases are the initial regression evidence. This is implementation provenance, not an independent certification of the MF-Tyre model.

The coefficient families, inflation-pressure effects, camber formulation and radii are described by I. J. M. Besselink, A. J. C. Schmeitz and H. B. Pacejka, “An improved Magic Formula/Swift tyre model that can handle inflation pressure changes”, Vehicle System Dynamics 48(sup1), 337–352 (2010), DOI https://doi.org/10.1080/00423111003748088. The accepted manuscript is available at https://pure.tue.nl/ws/files/3139488/677330157969510.pdf. That paper explicitly excludes turn-slip extensions; their provenance here is the supplied implementation, not that manuscript. The supplied source also identifies Pacejka, *Tire and Vehicle Dynamics*, appendix A3, pp. 617–619, for the rolling-radius and vertical-deflection equations. The project's explicit evaluation policies below resolve numerical cases not fixed by those source comments. Full commercial MF-Tyre equivalence is not claimed.

## Evaluation order

1. Condition FZ, SA, SR, IA and P against the supplied limits; preserve raw inputs and emit flags. No contact on the loaded side gives exactly zero physical outputs.
2. Form `dfz = (Fz - FNOMIN*LFZ0)/(FNOMIN*LFZ0)` and `dpi = (P - NOMPRES)/NOMPRES`. LONGVL and NOMPRES are positive reference values. LFZ0 is the source spelling (digit zero); the loader also accepts the historical LFZO spelling if values agree.
3. Determine effective rolling radius with the bounded fixed-point calculation; derive wheel speed from `(1 + kappa)*Vx/Re`.
4. With turn slip enabled, derive spin slip `phi` from turn slip, wheel speed and inclination, and apply the zeta factors. With it disabled the zeta factors are their ordinary steady-state values.
5. Compute pure longitudinal and lateral forces, with pressure-dependent friction and stiffness, speed-dependent friction scaling, camber stiffness and lateral shift.
6. In combined mode apply the cosine weighting functions and induced lateral shift. Compute pneumatic trail and residual aligning torque, then `Mz = -t*Fy_prime + Mzr + s*Fx`.
7. Compute overturning and rolling-resistance moments and loaded radius. Return the selected pure/combined forces, moments, radii and flags.
8. Compute tangent stiffness by forward-mode differentiation of the same force kernels. Secants use raw slips, with the tangent at zero. Peak-slip channels use the existing first-stationary-point search.

## Deliberate differences from the source

- The source scalar dispatcher interchanges SA/SR for FXP and MZP, and both dispatchers feed SA to the effective-radius equation. The port calls equations using their declared physical arguments.
- The source `s` function computes spin slip and then passes that as turn slip into `fy`. The port passes the original turn-slip input once.
- Curvature factors follow the existing repository rule `E <= 1`; numerical failures use its checked-evaluation error contract rather than source exceptions.
- Denominators use the repository's sign-preserving epsilon guards where they may reach zero. Norms use their right directional derivative at the origin, including nonzero friction-speed scaling and shifted force curves, avoiding `0/0` in dual arithmetic. The turn-slip lateral reduction is algebraically expanded before differentiating so its finite slope survives the square-root term at zero spin slip.
- Radius convergence uses a tolerance of 1e-6 m and at most 100 updates. Non-convergence yields nonfinite radius-dependent results and checked evaluation reports NUMERICAL.
- The input speed and pressure domains are strictly positive. Turn slip is a construction-time option, disabled by default. Its input TS is in inverse metres.
- All coefficient citations and equation provenance remain in this research note, rather than in code or fixtures.

## Characteristics and limits

The eight characteristic names and definitions are identical to those of MF 5.x. Derivatives include enabled turn-slip corrections. A clamped slip has zero tangent with respect to its raw value. Unloaded rows give zero non-peak characteristics and NaN peak slips. Pressure limits add PRESMIN/PRESMAX and P_CLAMPED. Vx below VXLOW is flagged without a low-speed taper.

## Evidence boundaries

The original fixture checks are deliberately retained as source regressions, not labelled as independent reference data. Corrected dispatcher behavior is tested directly. Any discrepancies caused by documented numerical guards or corrected argument flow are identified separately from regression failures.

## Coordinate gauges

The existing repository converts by two algebraic coefficient gauges, leaving IA unchanged. TS likewise stays unchanged: spin slip is invariant under these gauges. This is not a full physical vector-coordinate transformation. The vertical gauge also negates VERTICAL_STIFFNESS and the additional QSX terms so rolling radii and overturning moment retain their declared parity. For the turn-slip residual angle, the acos argument includes sgn(FNOMIN), preserving the canonical positive-load expression and vertical-gauge invariance.

At zero turn slip the residual-angle acos ratio uses the analytic small-TS limit of its numerator and denominator; its argument is clamped to [-1, 1]. The spin stiffness term uses the additive QDZ8 + QDZ9*dfz grouping, correcting the source product typo. These are explicit port policies and are covered separately from rounded source vectors.

## Spin-stiffness correction and regression references

The source residual amplitude contains the camber polynomial `[(QDZ8 + QDZ9*dfz) + (QDZ10 + QDZ11*dfz)*abs(gamma)]*gamma`. Its zero-camber linear coefficient is the sum `QDZ8 + QDZ9*dfz`, not their product. The port uses this additive grouping in the source spin-stiffness expression and groups the higher camber term consistently. This correction is derived algebraically from the supplied formulation; the pressure paper does not verify the turn-slip extension.

An independently compiled copy of the source, with only this grouping correction and the moment-arm turn-slip argument correction, gives the following aligning moments for the three supplied enabled-turn-slip cases (Fz=5000 N, Vx=27 m/s, P=180000 Pa, SR=0.1, TS=0.05 m^-1):

| SA [rad] | IA [rad] | Corrected source Mz [N m] |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0872665 | 0.0261799 | -8.895832935751637 |
| -0.0872665 | -0.0261799 | 2.306454054356660 |
| 0.0872665 | -0.0261799 | -12.748089423182599 |

These are source regression references with documented corrections, not independent model certification. The small reference tolerance accommodates the port's explicit sign-preserving guards. Independent algebraic tests also reduce the force equations to `D*sin(C*atan(B*slip))` with zero curvature and shifts, calculate the pressure multipliers directly, and verify right-hand slopes at the friction-speed norm cusp using one-sided differences.

The overturning and rolling-resistance equations receive the caller-selected lateral/longitudinal force, matching the repository's uncombined-mode contract. The old solver's MX/MY dispatch always recomputed combined forces; combined-mode values remain unchanged. Loaded-radius deformation retains the source's combined-force, zero-TS argument convention because there was no existing repository radius contract.

Family-local absolute values use the right directional derivative `abs(seed)` at zero, including negative spin-slip seeds. This matters when TS exactly cancels the camber spin: applying a scalar sign convention to a negative seed would reverse the one-sided force slope. The existing MF 5.x dual absolute-value behavior is unchanged.

At a real cusp, a reflected input maps a right derivative to a left derivative. Consequently the usual derivative sign-parity table applies at differentiable operating points; it does not assert right-to-right parity at a zero-slip friction-speed cusp with a nonzero force offset. Force equivalence and exact coefficient-transform round trips still hold there. The chosen right-hand convention is explicit and tested rather than pretending that the curve has a two-sided derivative.

The residual-angle clamp preserves NaN inputs so checked evaluation can report failures. At zero parking amplitude and zero spin stiffness, a zero limiting numerator uses the neutral ratio zero; a nonzero numerator uses its signed infinite ratio before clamping to the acos domain. No arbitrary finite denominator substitutes a physically different limiting ratio.
